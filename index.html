About this month: why, what we learn, and the choices behind it

Why this work

Cancer drugs are usually tested on cells grown flat on plastic. In a tumour, cells sit inside a soft or stiff 3D tissue that changes how they grow, move and respond to drugs. Our goal is to build 3D hydrogels that reproduce these tumour conditions, so drugs can be tested in a more realistic setting.

Before designing those gels, we need a baseline: do head and neck cancer cells grow in our GelMA, do they change the gel around them, and which drug doses work on them. This month answers exactly that.

Why these drugs

Cisplatin
The standard chemotherapy for head and neck cancer. It damages DNA and kills dividing cells. We use it as the reference drug to compare how cells respond in 2D and, later, in 3D gels.
Defactinib
Blocks FAK, a protein cells use to grip the surrounding matrix and move. Here it is a tool, not a treatment: if migration drops with defactinib, we know our migration test measures what it should.
Mitomycin C
Stops cell division. Used only in the scratch assay, so that the wound closes because cells move, not because they multiply.

Why these cells

FaDu
Squamous cell carcinoma of the hypopharynx, HPV-negative, fast-growing (doubling ≈30–50 h). Widely used, with published cisplatin data in 2D and 3D, so our results can be compared with others.
Detroit 562
Pharyngeal carcinoma taken from a metastasis, HPV-negative, slow-growing (doubling ≈90 h), with a PIK3CA mutation. Its metastatic origin makes it relevant for migration and invasion, and it behaves differently from FaDu.

What these two lines do not solve

  • Neither comes from the mouth: oral-cavity lines will still be needed.
  • Both are HPV-negative, so HPV-positive tumours are not covered.
  • Long-established lines, adapted to plastic: they do not capture the variation between patients.
  • Cancer cells alone: no fibroblasts or immune cells, which also shape drug response in a real tumour.
  • Two lines show a trend, not a general rule for all head and neck cancers.

Reagents

Cisplatin
Stock 1 mg/mL (3.3 mM) in 0.9% NaCl. Never DMSO. Light-protected tube at room temperature, use within one month.
Defactinib
Stock 10 mM in DMSO, single-use aliquots, frozen.
Mitomycin C
Per supplier. Cytotoxic and mutagenic: biosafety cabinet, gloves, chemo waste.
WST-1
Enough for all 2D plates and all 3D proliferation gels of the month.
GelMA
One single lot for the whole month. Record source, lot number, degree of methacrylation, light source, intensity and exposure time.

Why one GelMA lot: the same 7.5% GelMA gave a 4-fold different stiffness between two lots in our previous work. Changing lot in the middle of the month makes the results impossible to compare.

What is the highest defactinib dose that does not harm the cells during a scratch assay?

Groups

Replicates
3 wells per group; 3 independent runs.
Decision rule
Safe dose = the highest concentration whose mean viability stays within 10% of vehicle in all runs. Use it, plus the next lower concentration, in the scratch assay (tab B).

Why: if the dose weakens the cells, the wound closes less because there are fewer cells, not because they migrate less.

Runs

B · Scratch assay with defactinib: what and why

Does defactinib slow the migration of these cells? Two steps, in this order.

Step 1 · Mitomycin C check

What it is: mitomycin C stops cell division, so in the scratch assay the wound closes only by migration. This check finds the dose that stops division without killing or detaching the cells. Run it once per cell line, before the first scratch assay.

Day 0Seed to full confluence24-well plate, no scratch
Day 1Mitomycin C 2 h, wash ×2count cells (or WST-1) right after
Day 2 (24 h)Count cells againand look at the morphology

Pass: cell number does not increase over 24 h, and cells stay attached and spread. Use the lowest dose that passes in Step 2. Fail: cells round up or detach, so drop to 5 µg/mL or to 1 h.

Step 2 · Scratch assay

Day 1Mitomycin C, scratch, washdose from Step 1
Day 1Add groups, image 0 hsame positions every time
Until closedImage againuntil vehicle wounds are nearly closed
Scratch
One straight line per well with a 200 µL pipette tip, held vertical, same pressure each time. Wash twice with PBS to remove floating cells.
Imaging
Phase contrast, same positions just above and below the marker line, same magnification throughout.
Analysis
Open wound area in ImageJ (Wound Healing Size Tool or manual outline). Send raw images too.
